
TEMPLATE LAYOUT RULES (local test, added after the rules above; where they differ, these win):
- Subjective, Past Medical History and Objective are flat lists of short, self-contained lines. Every line must make sense on its own (never a bare fragment such as "Almost every day" or "Whatever brand is available": say what it is about, e.g. "Takes Nurtec almost every day").
- Subjective is only what the patient reports. Never write a decision of the clinician there ("Takes before surgery", "Will start...", "Advised..."): decisions go in the plan.
- Objective lines are "Label: value" (for example "BP: 143/88 mmHg (initial), 130/80 mmHg (repeat)", "Weight: 81 kg", "Chest: clear", "Bloods (Aug): Hb 150; glucose 6.3 (high); LDL 2.7"). Only what the clinician measured, examined or reviewed in this visit. A reading the patient says they took at home ("160/100 last night") is Subjective, not Objective. One line per measurement or test; never the same value twice.
- Past Medical History: one short status line per condition or medication ("Hypertension, on Atacand 16 mg daily", "Cholesterol, on Lipitor, stopped because of leg and muscle pain"). Leave out anything that is not medical background (hobbies, work events, conferences, what the patient bakes or cooks).
- NEVER invent. Do not name a problem, diagnosis, procedure or consent that the clinician did not say. A drug prescribed with no reason given is written under the problem it was discussed with, or under "Medication prescription"; never turn a drug into a condition (no "Depression" or "Anxiety" because of an antidepressant). A procedure the clinician only offered ("I can freeze it") is not a procedure performed. Do not write "informed consent obtained" unless it was said.
- Keep ALL the detail. Flat lists are not shorter lists: every symptom, duration, denial, medication, test result and plan item in the transcript stays, one short self-contained line each.
- Past Medical History never contains advice, plans or what was decided in this visit.
- Assessment & Plan: problem titles are plain, specific names in the clinician's words ("Elevated cholesterol", "Elevated blood pressure", "Borderline high blood sugar", "Lymphocytosis", "Pre-operative assessment"). Never a verb, drug or sentence in the title.
- If a procedure, surgery or anaesthesia is discussed, write a problem "Pre-operative assessment" (or "Pre-anaesthesia assessment") with the date of the surgery, what must be done before it, and any condition the clinician set (for example "Surgery will not go ahead if blood pressure stays high").
- Drug verbs must be exact: "Resume <drug>" when the patient had stopped or was not taking it and the clinician tells them to take it again; "Start <drug>" only for a drug that is new; "Continue <drug>" when the patient is taking it and keeps taking it; "Stop <drug>"; "Increase/Reduce <drug> to ...". Keep the frequency exactly as spoken ("at least 4 times weekly" is not "every day"). Keep the reason for stopping when the patient gave one.
- A verb goes only in front of a drug or treatment. Diet, lifestyle and monitoring are written as plain instructions ("Reduce sugar intake", "Avoid cookies", "Recheck blood pressure at home"), never as "Start Sugar pills" or "Continue Dietary control".
- One plan line per action. Each drug appears once per problem, in one line with its full dose and frequency. Do not repeat a drug in another form ("Lexapro 10 mg" and "Lexapro (escitalopram) 10 mg" are one line). Do not copy history or lab values into the plan.
- A follow-up is a time and a purpose ("Follow-up in 3 months to recheck bloods and blood pressure"). Say it once.
